NORTHSTAR / HOME DASHBOARD
01 — OVERVIEW

A little more clarity.
A lot less worry.

Northstar is a concept for a mobile banking experience built around a simple idea: money tools should help people feel capable, not behind. I designed a calm dashboard that makes daily finances easier to understand, while leaving room for long-term goals to feel possible.

This self-directed project explores the end-to-end product design process, from identifying anxieties around financial apps to prototyping a more human alternative.

02 — THE PROBLEM

Money apps show the numbers.
They don’t always show a way forward.

People have more financial data than ever, but knowing what to do with it is another matter. Many banking experiences put transactions and charts first, leaving people to connect the dots on their own.

“
I don’t need another graph. I want to know if I’m doing okay.
— RESEARCH PARTICIPANT, 28

The challenge: help people make sense of their everyday money without judgment, jargon, or another layer of complexity.

03 — RESEARCH & INSIGHTS

Start with how it feels,
not just what it does.

I spoke with six people in their twenties and thirties about how they check in on their money, what makes them feel in control, and where existing apps fall short. I mapped the recurring moments of friction and used them to shape three design principles.

01

Context over raw data

A number becomes useful when people can see what changed and why.

02

Progress without pressure

Goals should feel flexible and encouraging, not like another score to miss.

03

Reassurance in the details

Plain language and predictable patterns make a sensitive task feel safer.

Research note: this is an educational portfolio project with a small exploratory sample, not a financial product or validated study.

04 — WIREFRAMES & ITERATION

Make the next step
easy to find.

Early sketches tested different ways to balance a reassuring overview with useful detail. In the first round, goals competed with the account balance. Testing quick prototypes showed that people wanted an immediate read on their money, then a clear path to explore it.

Keep the balance
easy to scan.Move goals below
the daily overview.
WIREFRAME STUDY / ROUND 02

I moved the balance and its recent context to the top, grouped savings into approachable “spaces,” and made secondary actions quieter. This gave the screen a clearer reading order without hiding the deeper information.

05 — FINAL SOLUTION

Financial clarity,
with a softer edge.

The final dashboard puts a calm snapshot first: a clear balance, a small progress signal, and a short view of what matters next. Warm neutrals, a restrained green accent, and plain-language labels keep the experience approachable without disguising the information.
